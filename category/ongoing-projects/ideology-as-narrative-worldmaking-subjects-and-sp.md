<p><strong>Book Project</strong> by&nbsp;Milan Vuka&scaron;inović, University of Uppsala<br />
<strong>Overview</strong><br />
Ideology is the most often used non-defined concept in Byzantine and Serbian Medieval studies. This book conceives it as performative narrative discourse, as stories that made active social subjects and the space they lived in. It examines the period of the half-century after the Crusaders&rsquo; conquest and colonization of Byzantine territories. It reconciles modern theoretical considerations and the interpretation of medieval texts. The author reads the narratives from Nicaea, Epiros, and Serbia in texts ranging from imperial orations and hagiographies, to monastic donations and juridical opinions, not as representations of events, but as active social practices that created the world. Using the notions of how individuals become subjects and produce space, the study proposes ways in which specific emperors, bishops, monks, and peasants interacted as storytellers, heroes, and social agents. It argues for diversified social and political agencies and marginalizes standard narratives of Byzantine fragmentation and Serbian independence.<br />
