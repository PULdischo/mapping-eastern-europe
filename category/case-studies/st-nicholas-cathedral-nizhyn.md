*By Maksym Yaremenko | NationalUniversity of Kyiv-Mohyla Academy*

**Description**

	St. Nicholas Cathedral in Nizhyn is the oldest stone cruciform (cross-shaped) five-domed, nine-chambered church on the left bank of the Dnieper River, and an example of early Ukrainian Baroque architecture. The church was built by emulating the forms of log structures used in the construction of local wooden churches. 

The cathedral was built using grooved brick between 1655 and 1658 with the support of the Zolotarenkos, a Cossack chief officer family, whose representatives were Nizhyn colonels. The plan of the church is in the shape of an equilateral cross, with smaller chambers built between its arms. This gives the structure a pyramidal shape (hence the nine-chamber design). The cathedral’s walls are plastered and whitewashed both inside and out. None of the wall paintings have survived. In addition to the iconostasis, there are references to another icon. According to the Croatian Catholic theologian Yurii Kryzhanich, who passed through Nizhyn in 1659, an icon was mounted above the cathedral’s entrance. The founder, Vasyl Zolotarenko, had brought the icon back from a military campaign in Belarussian lands. In the western section, on either side of the main entrance, there are galleries with arched openings. These are interpreted as special lodges reserved for honourable parishioners. All facades feature a similar artistic design – they are decorated with panels of various geometric shapes. The cathedral once had three altars: in honor of St. Nicholas, Archangel Michael, and the Three Holy Hierarchs.

The founders of the church, the Nizhyn colonels Ivan (d. 1655) and Vasyl (d. 1663) Zolotarenkos had burgher’s origins and entered the political arena thanks to the Cossack Revolution (1648–1657) led by Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytskyi. This revolution led to the creation of the Hetmanate – an autonomous state made of parts of the Ukrainian territories. Ivan and Vasyl’s sister was Khmelnytskyi’s wife. The brothers also distinguished themselves in a number of military campaigns during the revolution. The Nizhyn Regiment, commanded by the Zolotarenko brothers, was one of the largest administrative-territorial units of the Hetmanate.

**Significance**

The St. Nicholas Cathedral in Nizhyn was built in the center of the Old Town on the site of a former wooden church of the same dedication. The names of its builders are unknown, but scholars have suggested that they were local craftsmen. Among the surviving sources for this early history, and the most reliable ones, is a 19th-century historical and statistical description of St. Nicholas Cathedral by Gumilevskiy, which states that there was a silver cross on the altar in the cathedral. The cross bore an inscription indicating that it had been donated to the church in 1606. Another source is a description of the church from 1832, which, based on the publication dates of the oldest liturgical books, dates the construction of the church to approximately the 1630s. There is also a description from the late 18th century in which, relying on the testimony of local elders, the author likewise mentions a wooden church.

The cathedral served as a model for several stone churches built in the Hetmanate in the decades following its construction: Holy Trinity Cathedral of the Holy Trinity Monastery in Hustyn, St. George Cathedral of the Vydubyckyi St. Michael’s Monastery in Kyiv, the Church of All Saints at the Economic Gate of the Kyiv-Caves Lavra, the Holy Dormition Cathedral in Novhorod-Siverskyi, the St. Catherine’s Church in Chernihiv, the Holy Dormition Church in Nizhyn (18th century), and others. 

In 1734, a carved wooden Baroque iconostasis, painted by master Vasyl Reklynsky, was installed in the church. The iconostasis was destroyed around the 1930s. In the second half of the 18th century, the cathedral suffered from fires several times. Contemporary descriptions succinctly summarize the damage caused by fires: “In many places, it requires considerable repair”. And the plaster had partially fallen off on the inside and completely on the outside. As a result of the 19th-century reconstruction, the shapes of the cathedral’s domes were altered to be more elongated and part of the ornamentation on the facades was lost.

The cathedral was closed in the early 1930s during the Soviet anti-religious campaign. It also suffered damage during World War II (cracks in the walls, destroyed domes, vaults, etc.). The restoration required significant funds. Local authorities denounced a lack of funds for the restoration and proposed demolishing this church together with other monuments and landmarks in Nizhyn. They also argued that churches detracted from the socialist cityscape. All of this took place during Khrushchev’s anti-religious campaign. But by that time, the architects had already been examining the cathedral on behalf of the State Committee for Construction of the Ukrainian SSR,that is, the highest state authority. In 1963, the cathedral was designated a republican-level architectural monument, so it was no longer possible to demolish it. The severely damaged and shabby building was examined by restorers between 1957 and 1966. The cathedral was partially restored in 1966 (cornices and dome roofs) and fully restored between 1980 and 1990.
