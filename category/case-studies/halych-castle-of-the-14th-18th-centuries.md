*By Myroslav Voloshchuk | Vasyl Stefanyk Carpathian National University (Ivano-Frankivsk, Ukraine)*

**Description** 

Halych fortress was built in the first half of the 12th century, when the Rostislavovid dynasty first established themselves in this area, and is located in the same place where the future Halych castle would be built – on the mountain top. The first Prince of Halych was Ivan Vasylkovych. He ruled here until 1141. Archeologists have suggested that the fortification was originally made of wood, enclosed with ramparts which afterwards were replaced and strengthened with the  castle of the Princes of Halych during the 12th–14th centuries. Probably near the Castle Mountain was located the court of famous Halych boyar Sudyslav († after 1234), looted by the troop of Volhynian Prince Danylo Romanovych in spring 1230. In the middle of the 14th century Halych castle was rebuilt and after 1387 became a ruling center of the local captain and castellan. Later the castle turned into the administrative center of the Halych *starosta* within the Ruthenian voivodeship of the Kingdom of Poland, and after 1569 – the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. It was used as a prison, and defensive center of the Halych land, especially during the 15th–17th centuries against the Vlachs, Tatars, Turks, Cossacks and later the Muscovites. The main families which took care of the Halych castle were the Khodeсki and Potocki. Representatives of the Khodecki family actively held government positions in the Ruthenian and Podolia voivodeships of the Kingdom of Poland in the second half of the 15th – first third of the 16th century. Instead, from the end of the 16th century, the Potocki family became the greatest magnates of the Galician region and the Ruthenian Voivodeship. Halych castle partly lost its role after the construction of the Stanislav fortress in 1662. The new city, fortified with the latest military engineering achievements, was located between two rivers, and served as a more reliable protection for the surrounding lands. 

But Halych castle still played its role in the 18th century and even became the center of the organization of a separate confederation in 1768 on the eve of the First Division of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth in 1772. Then the castle had a triangular shape, covered an area of ​​1,7 hectares, had 2 terraces, 3 brick towers at the corners, 9 storerooms, an archive, a chancellery, courtroom, and a chapel dedicated to St. Catherine.

**Significance** 

During its history Halych castle withstood many sieges. The first mention of the plundering of Halych in the 14th century can be found in Jan Długosz’s *Annals* in 1353, when Lithuanian Prince Liubartas (r. cca 1312/5 – until 1386) conquered the city. Later the castle was sieged by the Polish and Lithuanian troops in Summer 1387. Twice Halych castle was granted by the kings of Poland as a separate possession for the rulers of Moldova: in 1391 – to Prince Peter Muşat and in 1436 – to Prince Ilya. In 1490 Halych castle defended itself from the army of Moldavian leader Mukha and in 1509 – Moldavian Prince Bogdan II the Blind. Around 1520 the castle was rebuilt, presumably using stone masonry. The oldest inventory of Halych castle is dated to September 17, 1582. But afterwards the castle burned at the end of the 16th century. 

The renovated and rebuilt fortification withstood up to 29 attacks by the Ottomans and Tatars between 1590 and 1633. The most destructive being the attacks in 1594, 1612, and 1624. In the times of Cossack uprising led by hetman Bogdan Khmelnitsky (1648-57) Halych castle was sieged by the troops of colonel Semen Vysochan, and later conquered by Bogdan Khmelnitsky in 1649. That’s why the most famous local magnate Andriy Potocki conducted the rebuilding of the castle in 1658. He gathered 42 thousand zloty (cca 1260 kg of silver) for the rebuilding of the fortress. The plan of reconstruction was developed by the Burgundian engineer Francois Corrazini. The reconstructed castle was attacked again in 1676 during the war between the Ottoman Empire, the Crimean khanate and the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. The troops led by Ibrahim Pasha Shaytan (1595–1685) conquered the stronghold, without a fight. The Polish commandant Lakhovskyy, having no hope of defending the castle, opened the castle’s gate and gave the castle to the Ottomans. Then they blasted the part with the defensive walls and towers. Soon the castle was again rebuilt. At the same time since 1662 between two rivers called Bystrycia (both are tributaries of the Dniester river) existed another new city-fortress Stanislav. This fortress successfully withstood the Turkish siege of 1672. That’s why the Halych castle gradually lost the main defensive role and became desolate. 

In accordance with the order of the voivode in 1767, part of the surviving walls and other materials were taken to build the city Halych. The ruins of the ancient fortress stood above Halych up to the end of the 20th century. Only one tower (the Noble Tower) and fragments of the chapel of St. Catherine have survived from that time. The rest of the castle walls were covered with vegetation. During the Soviet times, researching the castle was not a priority.

After the creation of the National reserve “*Ancient Halych*” in 1994, Halych Castle became the largest and least explored monument. Minor archaeological research of the castle was firstly conducted by Dr. Yuriy Lukomsky in the late 1990s. But the main, comprehensive archaeological research began in 2022. During this time, despite the Russian-Ukrainian war, the team of the National reserve “*Ancient Halych*”, as well as students from several universities of Ivano-Frankivsk and volunteers excavated the Entrance Tower, blown up by the Turks in 1676. Archaeologists managed to get to the lower tier of this tower, previously unknown from any sources. The territory of the Halych castle gradually turned into a venue for historical festivals and other performances. The castle became the venue for the 2023 festive session of the Ivano-Frankivsk Regional Council on the occasion of the 1125th anniversary of the first mention of the city of Halych. 

Currently, the archaeological excavations are going on, gradually transforming Halych Castle into an even more important research and tourist site.
