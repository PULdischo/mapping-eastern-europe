*By Myroslav Voloshchuk | Vasyl Stefanyk Carpathian National University (Ivano-Frankivsk, Ukraine)*

**Description** 

The Church of the Nativity of Christ in Halych was built in the 14th or 15th century. According to Joseph Pelenskyy, there was a wooden church on the site during the 12th century, after which the new brick church was named. Unfortunately, the layout of the medieval church is still unknown. 

From the 15th century, the church served as a parish church and belonged to the artisans of Halych, especially the shoemakers’ guild. This guild arose in Halych after the city’s transition to Magdeburg law *terminus ante quem* 1367. The ethnic composition of the guild and the number of members remain unknown. The church was located at the foot of Halych Castle, where royal officials had resided since the 14th century. In 1550, the city magistrate issued a charter for the shoemakers’ guild, according to which “the guild had its altar in the Halych parish church.” In 1593, the King of Poland Sigismund III (1566–1632) approved the charter of the Halych pottery workshop, according to which the Church of the Nativity of Christ became their property. An Orthodox brotherhood was created for the church, which took care of the temple and was in opposition to the local bishop of Halych Gedeon Balaban (1530–1607). 

During the 17th and 18th centuries, the church repeatedly suffered from fires and destruction caused by hostile invasions, mainly by the Tatars and the Turks. According to the eparchial visitation of 1740 of the Lviv Metropolitan Consistory of St. George, initiated by the Metropolitan of Kyiv, Galicia and all Rus’ Athanasius Szeptycki, one of the brick churches of Halych was the Church of the Nativity of Christ. There was an altar in the church, on which lay a wooden cross. Several lanterns lay nearby. The floor was made of planks. The church also contained two silver goblets, one of which was gilded, silver spoons and some other silver items, and towels made of Persian cloth. In the nearby bell tower, there were three bells. 

Today, the church has  a central nave and two side aisles, a transept, a porch, and one dome. The walls are finished with cornices, the roof is gabled. The overall dimensions of the church are 29.5 × 16.6 m. The thickness of the walls is 1.55 m. The foundations are very deep. The Church of the Nativity of Christis an architectural monument of national importance and a part of the National Reserve “*Ancient Halych*”.

**Significance**

Until the 1930s, the Church of the Nativity of Christ was identified with the Assumption cathedral of Halych, built in the middle of the 12th century. This opinion was expressed by the scholar Antin Petrushevych for the first time in the second half of the 19th century. However, the results of archaeological research conducted by Isidor Sharanevych, Joseph Pelenskyy, and by Yaroslav Pasternak in 1936 confirmed that the Assumption cathedral of Halych was located on the Krylos hillfort. 

Since the church was repeatedly destroyed and rebuilt, most of the funds for construction work were paid for by the local priests. Thus, the initiator of the repair and restoration of the church in 1825 was the parish priest K. Levytskyy. Financial support was also provided by Metropolitan M. Levytskyy. During the mentioned restoration, the buttresses were removed from the northern and southern sides, and the previously missing choirs were placed in the narthex. The church was covered with a gable roof with a Baroque pediment.      

The next stage of renovation work came during the time of priest Mykola Vynnytskyy. Between 1904 and 1906, he secured considerable funds for the renovation of the church from the Halych Sejm (the so called Diet of the Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria, and of the Grand Duchy of Cracow, was the regional assembly, located in Lviv from 1861 until the dissolution of Austrian-Hungarian monarchy in 1918). He was the first to involve specialists from the Lviv Regional Circle of Antiquities in the repair and restoration work of the monument. During the work, the old, decaying wooden bell tower above the portal was dismantled, a new large dome was installed, and the inside of the church was covered with new plaster. The church was consecrated by Metropolitan Andrey Sheptytskyy. However, since priest Mykola Vynnytsky was aligned with the Muscovite milieu in his political views, during the First World War 127 residents of Halych, led by their priest, were sent to the Austrian concentration camp Thalerhof. Only a few managed to return home. In 1929, priest Mykola Vynnytskyy, in memory of the arrested Halych Inhabitants, erected a cross in the courtyard of the Church of the Nativity of Christ, which has survived to this day. 

In 1960, the interior of the church was renovated. This project of the restoration and new paintings (which survive until today) was carried out by the artists Mykhailo and Petro Shchupaks. In 1963, the Church of the Nativity was included in the register of movable monuments of Ukraine with the status of a monument of national importance, under the care and protection of the state. In 1970 and 1975, Lviv architect Ivan Mogytych re-examined the church, carried out a graphic reconstruction of its original dimensions, and also concluded that the structure of the church was similar to the Armenian Cathedral in Lviv. The mentioned Armenian church was built there between 1363 and 1370, founded by an Armenian merchant from Caffa in Crimea.

On the eve of the celebration of the 1100th anniversary of Halych in 1997–1998, Lviv specialists examined the walls of the church, renovated the entrance in the western part of the church, preserved part of its foundations, and covered the roof with tiles. At the same time, in 1998, a model of medieval Halych and its churches and monasteries, created by the Lviv archaeologist Yuriy Lukomskyy, was installed in the courtyard of the church. 

Dismantled in 1825, the buttresses became the subject of interest and new research by employees of the National Reserve “*Ancient Halych*” in 2017. The foundations of the buttresses were excavated from the north side of the church, and after their examination, they were displayed under glass in a museum setting.
