*By Myroslav Voloshchuk | Vasyl Stefanyk Carpathian National University (Ivano-Frankivsk, Ukraine)*

**Description** 

The Orthodox Assumption church in Krylos (former Princely and Royal center of Halych) was built in 1579 very close to the ruins of the Orthodox Assumption Cathedral of the 12th century. That’s why the building material (stone blocks and artworks) for the church was mostly taken from the destroyed cathedral. The first liturgy in the church dates back to 1584. The founder of the Assumption church was one of the most famous representatives of the local elite – Marko Shumlyanskyy. Probably he was the descendant of the Halych nobility of the 12th–13th centuries. Above the galleries in the church the stone was preserved with a coat of arms. 

The Assumption church general measures are: length – 25,8 m, width – 13,4 m, high – 15,7 m. The written sources confirm that the church was a four-column, three-apses, one central dome construction encircled with galleries from three sides and a rectangular narthex in the west. At the end of the 16th century, the church was surrounded by brick defensive walls with four towers at the corners. This church was smaller than the Assumption Cathedral, because there was no need to rebuild such a large temple. The Assumption church was a key monument in the structure of the Orthodox Bishopric of Halych, Lviv and Kamyanets until 1700. 

The temple was totally ruined during the Tatar-Turkish invasion in 1676. The restoration of the church was undertaken at the expense of bishop Joseph Shumlyanskyy (d. 1708) and was finished in 1702.

The Assumption church is an architectural monument of national importance and part of the National Reserve “*Ancient Halych*”.

**Significance**

Unlike the Assumption Cathedral, the Assumption church has survived to our day, although in an extensively rebuilt  form. In the Gospel that was presented to the church by bishop Joseph Shumlyanskyy in 1679, there was a record about a terrifying Turkish-Tatar invasion of the Halych land in 1676. The temple was robbed and destroyed. Many of the inhabitants of Krylos were killed and taken in captivity. In 1699, the restoration works of the church began and were completed in 1702. 

The temple preserved many graffiti, especially from the end of the 16th century and its foundation. The western portal of the church is of remarkable artistic value due to the sculptural stone decoration of the Dormition of the Virgin Mary in the tympanum. The northern portal is decorated with fretted stone brackets and completed by a three-cornered fronton on the tympana, where the emblem of the restorer of the church is placed containing the Episcopal coat of arms with the letters of “I. Sh. E. L. G. K. – A–V”, which are decrypted as “Joseph Shumlyanskyy, a bishop of Lviv, Halych and Kamyanets – 1702”. During the rebuilding of the church, Joseph Shumlyanskyy commissioned and placed a gravestone over the grave of his glorious ancestor – the founder of the Assumption Cathedral, Marko Shumlyanskyy. It was moved in 1991 near the central entrance to the church, and nowadays it is in the narthex. On the white-stone slab is engraved in Old Ukrainian: “Here is buried Marko Shumlyanskyy, the founder of the Assumption church, the descendant of Big and Small Shumlyany, who lived to 85 and was legitimized by Polish King Sigismund…” 

As it is known from the written sources, the roof of the church also was significantly damaged in the first half of the 18th century, possibly due to an earthquake. In the years of World War I the church roof fell off because of artillery bombardments. Then the roof was restored in the model of a basilican architectural style. At the beginning of the 1990s the temple was restored to its original form – the works were finished in 2001. During the restoration the archaeological investigations offered new and rich material. The team led by Dr. Yuriy Lukomskyy found numerous fragments of wall constructions from the 12th century Assumption Cathedral, elements of the stone decoration of the temple, and fragments of the fresco painting, which offer new evidence for the  original appearance and internal decorating of the former Cathedral. 

The ecclesiastical and canonical subordination of the Assumption Church during the 16th–20th centuries had changed. From the 16th century to the Brest Union in 1596 the Orthodox Assumption church was in the structure of the Kyivan Metropolis. Later the Orthodox hierarchy of the bishopric of Halych, Lviv and Kamyanets, led by bishop Gedeon Balaban was directly subordinated to the Patriarch of Constantinople as a separate exarchate. The Kyivan Metropolis was renovated in 1620, but after its annexation to the Moscow Patriarchate in 1686, bishop Joseph Shumlyanskyy, on behalf of the Orthodox bishops, appealed to the Moscow Patriarch Joachim with a request to establish the Halych Metropolis under the Moscow Patriarch on the territory of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. However, it was impossible. That’s why on the turn of the 17th–18th centuries most of the clergy joined the Uniate church. 

Currently the temple is the permanent residence of the Primate of the Ukrainian Greek-Catholic church. Its remarkable iconostasis is the work of the prominent Ukrainian painter of the beginning of the 20th century, Andriy Monastyrskyy. He is also the painter of the Miraculous Icon of the Divine Mother of Krylos. Interestingly, the northern wall of the church's narthex preserves an original 12th-century relief of a fairy-tale creature (probably a dragon). This fragment was built into the wall of the church from the remains of the Assumption Cathedral.

Traditions of the All-Ukrainian Patriarch Pilgrimage to the Halych Miraculous Icon of Divine Mother in Krylos and the Assumption church have been going on since 2012. This holy place attracts pilgrims from different parts of Ukraine. And the central temple of the Krylos hillfort gradually turned into the seat of the head of the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church.
